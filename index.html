Atrial fibrillation detection from PPG

Wearable-compatible cardiac screening without ECG dependency

Atrial fibrillation is the most common sustained arrhythmia and is frequently asymptomatic, so it is often found only after a stroke. Smartwatches and armbands already record photoplethysmography continuously — the open question is whether that signal alone supports reliable rhythm classification once motion artefact, poor perfusion and sensor contact loss are allowed in.

The work builds machine learning models for AF detection on PPG, trained against beat-level labels transferred from simultaneously recorded ECG. Getting that transfer right required a synchronisation stage of its own: a sync-point clock-correction algorithm that removes device offset and gradual drift, and an offset scan with beat-by-beat pulse pairing that recovers the optimal temporal shift. The target is a low false-positive rate, since alarm fatigue is what makes continuous monitoring fail in practice.
